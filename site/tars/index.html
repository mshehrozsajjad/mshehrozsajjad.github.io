01 / The confession

Two years of AI. Zero minutes of soldering.

I had spent two years building with AI, all of it safely behind a screen. Then I wanted to try hardware. I started from an open-source TARS with a 3D-printed body, added my own ideas for how it moves and what it senses, and held a soldering iron for the first time in my life.Two years into building with AI, I wanted to try hardware. Base: an open-source TARS project with a 3D-printed body. Added: my own ideas for motion and extra sensors.

The motion sensor went in. Nobody had warned it.I soldered in a motion sensor. It was my first time using a soldering iron.

NotesBuilt on TARS-AI, an open-source community project, and so is the 3D model on this page. Based on the mechanical puppet designs by Christopher Nolan, Nathan Crowley and the production team who first brought TARS to life in Interstellar; miniaturised CAD by Charlie Diaz, with modifications by the TARS-AI Community (CC BY-NC 4.0). Mine is a fan-made build.

02 / The walk

It fell over. A lot.

Walking on two slabs is harder than the film makes it look. So TARS taught itself to walk. Well, Claude did the experimenting. TARS did the falling.It kept falling over when it walked. Claude experimented with the motion until it found a walk that stays up.

Many tries later it walks, and it stays up.Result: it walks without falling.

04 / The bill

Then the bill arrived.

A robot that is always on is a robot that is always billing you. Keeping it running was super expensive, and the avatar was the hardest, priciest part. So the goal changed: run everything on my Mac, for free.Keeping it running all the time was expensive. The avatar was the hardest and costliest part. New goal: run the whole stack locally, at no cost. my wallet says hi

Free, private and offline, speech-to-text included. It can also control my appliances, which is either convenient or the first act of a film.Everything runs locally on a Mac, speech-to-text included: free and private. It can also control home appliances.

06 / What I learnt

What I learnt.

RevChangeWhy

  1. Always-on changes the maths.

    A demo is cheap. A thing that runs all day is not. The bill is what pushed me to the better design.

  2. The edge is closer than it looks.

    The part I assumed needed a data centre, the avatar, runs on the Mac on my desk.

  3. You can start hardware without knowing hardware.

    Begin with an open-source project, and let an AI run the experiments.
